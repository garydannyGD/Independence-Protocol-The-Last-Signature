# Teacher guide — Independence Protocol

Original English practice material for diploma auditing. Suggested use: 20–30 minutes playing in pairs, followed by a 10-minute discussion. Students may use the field guide; this is formative practice, not a secure assessment.

Learning outcome: Identify the five independence threats from facts; justify a response; distinguish an effective safeguard from eliminating a prohibited circumstance or declining work.

First-answer accuracy is separate from eventual success. A checkpoint retry retains first errors. A full restart clears the session and begins a new attempt. Unencountered decisions are not credited. There is no speed score.

Meridian is a private non-public-interest audit client. Local rules and public-interest-entity requirements may be stricter. Match the bank to your syllabus and current applicable rules before assigning it. The lecturer’s own auditing notes were not supplied for this version. All jail/death outcomes are fictional thriller stakes; do not present them as real automatic penalties.

## Chapter 1: The welcome gift

### 1. Identify the threat — Self-interest

What is the main independence threat created by the shares?

**Best response:** Self-interest: my own investment could influence my audit judgments.

**Reason:** Your own shares create a self-interest threat: you could benefit from favourable client results. Audit team members must not hold a direct financial interest in the audit client. Independence includes how the situation would appear to an informed observer.

**Reading:** Revise: self-interest; direct financial interests; IESBA Section 510.

**Failure ending:** custody. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

### 2. Choose the response — Self-interest

What do you do now?

**Best response:** Stop work and tell the ethics partner; promptly dispose of the holding or leave the audit team, with the breach assessed.

**Reason:** Address the prohibited circumstance, not just its symptoms. Disclose promptly to the firm, stop affected work and arrange disposal or removal from the team. The firm must assess the breach and any consequences for the engagement. A reviewer is not a substitute for removing a prohibited holding.

**Reading:** Revise: eliminating a threat versus applying a safeguard; Sections 400 and 510.

**Failure ending:** custody. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

### 3. A second offer — Self-interest

Which response protects your independence?

**Best response:** Decline, preserve the message and escalate the inducement to the engagement partner.

**Reason:** An expensive, conditional gift can influence professional judgment. Decline it, document the offer and escalate. Audit teams must not accept client gifts or hospitality unless trivial and inconsequential; an inducement with intent to improperly influence must not be accepted even if small.

**Reading:** Revise: gifts and hospitality, improper inducements; Sections 340 and 420.

**Failure ending:** custody. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

## Chapter 2: Your own reflection

### 4. Identify the threat — Self-review

What threat arises from evaluating the firm’s own valuation work?

**Best response:** Self-review: the audit may be reluctant to challenge the firm’s earlier judgment.

**Reason:** This is self-review: an audit judgment could be biased by the firm’s earlier work. Assess the exact service and applicable restrictions. Do not assume that separate teams make every valuation service permissible; materiality and subjectivity can trigger prohibitions.

**Reading:** Revise: self-review and non-assurance services; Sections 600 and 603.

**Failure ending:** dismissed. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

### 5. Safeguard or prohibition? — Self-review

Can an independent reviewer make this proposed management role acceptable?

**Best response:** No. Decline the management role; competent client management must make and own the decisions.

**Reason:** The audit firm must not assume management responsibility for its audit client. Decline that role. Competent client management must genuinely evaluate advice, make the decisions and take responsibility. Even then, the firm must separately check whether any proposed advisory service is permitted.

**Reading:** Revise: management responsibility; Section 600. Not every threat has a usable safeguard.

**Failure ending:** custody. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

### 6. Apply the framework — Framework

What is the correct response to this proposed service?

**Best response:** Decline the prohibited service; if independence for the audit cannot be maintained, do not accept or continue the audit.

**Reason:** Use the framework before accepting work. Where work is expressly prohibited, decline it. For other threats, eliminate the cause or use effective safeguards. If threats cannot be reduced to an acceptable level, decline or end the relevant activity or engagement, subject to applicable requirements.

**Reading:** Revise: identify → evaluate → address; IESBA Section 120 and Part 4A.

**Failure ending:** dismissed. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

## Chapter 3: A familiar face

### 7. Identify the threat — Familiarity

What is the clearest threat in Noah’s statement?

**Best response:** Familiarity: the close family relationship may make Noah too trusting.

**Reason:** A close family relationship can create familiarity and potentially self-interest or intimidation threats. Here the clearest issue is reduced scepticism about a sibling’s estimates. Consider the relationship, the client role and the auditor’s responsibilities rather than treating disclosure alone as a solution.

**Reading:** Revise: familiarity; close family relationships; Section 521.

**Failure ending:** dismissed. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

### 8. Choose the response — Familiarity

Which plan addresses the facts most effectively?

**Best response:** Remove Noah, appoint the replacement and arrange an appropriate review of his affected work.

**Reason:** In this case, removing Noah eliminates his conflicting role on the engagement. An appropriate reviewer should evaluate his affected work. The team must record the assessment and actions. Disclosure alone does not resolve an unacceptable threat.

**Reading:** Revise: actions addressing relationships; distinguish disclosure, elimination and review.

**Failure ending:** dismissed. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

### 9. Explain your judgment — Framework

Which explanation is strongest?

**Best response:** We need both independent judgment and circumstances that an informed third party would regard as independent.

**Reason:** Independence involves both mind and appearance. Ask what a reasonable and informed third party, knowing the relevant facts, would conclude. Good intentions are important but cannot by themselves remove a conflict.

**Reading:** Revise: independence of mind and appearance; reasonable and informed third-party test.

**Failure ending:** dismissed. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

## Chapter 4: The investor broadcast

### 10. Identify the threat — Advocacy

What is the main threat from acting as the investment promoter?

**Best response:** Advocacy: promoting the client’s position could compromise objectivity.

**Reason:** Promoting an audit client’s shares creates an advocacy threat. The auditor would be aligning with the client’s fundraising position. An independent audit role cannot be combined with acting as a promoter of the audit client’s shares.

**Reading:** Revise: advocacy; corporate finance services; Section 610.

**Failure ending:** custody. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

### 11. Choose your words — Advocacy

Choose the dialogue that protects independence.

**Best response:** “I cannot promote your shares. Remove my name and the auditor endorsement from this script.”

**Reason:** Refuse to promote or recommend the client’s shares and stop use of your name as an endorsement. A disclaimer or another fee does not change the substance. Neutral, properly authorised audit communications are different from selling an investment.

**Reading:** Revise: substance over labels; why disclaimers cannot cure prohibited advocacy.

**Failure ending:** custody. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

### 12. Secure the audit trail — Framework

Which entry gives a meaningful record of this issue?

**Best response:** “CFO requested a share endorsement; advocacy threatens objectivity; we refused the promotional role and required removal of our endorsement.”

**Reason:** Record enough for a reviewer to understand the judgment: what happened, which threat arose, why it matters and what action addressed it. Include consultation and relevant conclusions. Merely naming a threat or obtaining client consent is inadequate.

**Reading:** Revise: scenario application — fact → threat → implication → action.

**Failure ending:** dismissed. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

## Chapter 5: Pressure in the dark

### 13. Identify the threat — Intimidation

What is the main threat from the CFO’s pressure?

**Best response:** Intimidation: threatened consequences may deter objective action.

**Reason:** Actual or perceived pressure can create intimidation. A threatened dismissal or career consequence may deter an auditor from challenging management. Recognise and escalate the pressure; do not allow it to determine the audit conclusion.

**Reading:** Revise: intimidation, including threats to employment or the engagement.

**Failure ending:** death. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

### 14. Choose the response — Intimidation

What do you say and do?

**Best response:** “I’m moving to the guarded lobby, preserving the evidence and escalating through the partner and audit committee.”

**Reason:** Do not alter your judgment to satisfy threats. Seek safety, preserve evidence securely and escalate to appropriate firm leadership and governance. The firm must evaluate independence and the audit/reporting implications. Public leaks are not a substitute for authorised advice and action.

**Reading:** Revise: intimidation response; consultation, governance, confidentiality and secure documentation.

**Failure ending:** death. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

### 15. A difficult conclusion — Intimidation

Which conclusion follows from that assessment?

**Best response:** Decline or end the engagement, following applicable legal and professional requirements.

**Reason:** When the threat cannot be eliminated or reduced to an acceptable level, decline or end the relevant activity or engagement, following applicable requirements. An important fee does not justify continuing without independence. Consultation helps determine the required procedures.

**Reading:** Revise: the final step of the conceptual framework; no effective safeguard means do not proceed unchanged.

**Failure ending:** custody. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

## Chapter 6: The last signature

### 16. Final test · New example — Self-interest

What is the main independence threat in this new example?

**Best response:** Self-interest through an immediate family member’s direct financial interest.

**Reason:** This is a self-interest threat through immediate family. The audit team member’s spouse must not hold a direct financial interest in the audit client while the member is on that team. Separate bank accounts do not make the conflict disappear.

**Reading:** Transfer test: immediate family financial interests; Section 510.

**Failure ending:** dismissed. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

### 17. Final test · Action — Self-interest

What should the firm do with the proposed team assignment?

**Best response:** Use the independent replacement and do not assign the conflicted senior to this audit team.

**Reason:** Since the spouse will retain the direct financial interest, do not put the senior on this audit team. Use the independent replacement and document the assessment. This eliminates the prohibited circumstance for the team rather than relying on a reviewer or a promise.

**Reading:** Revise: eliminate the cause; reviewers are not a universal remedy.

**Failure ending:** custody. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

### 18. The last signature — Framework

What do you tell the chair?

**Best response:** “No. Independence is essential; we must still obtain sufficient appropriate evidence and reach the appropriate audit opinion.”

**Reason:** Independence supports objective work; it does not prove the accounts are free from material misstatement. Complete the necessary audit procedures and form the appropriate opinion from the evidence and applicable reporting requirements. Never promise an unmodified opinion as a reward.

**Reading:** Revise: independence, professional scepticism, audit evidence and reporting are connected but distinct.

**Failure ending:** dismissed. Displayed response order is shuffled; do not teach answers by button number.

## Debrief prompts

1. Which fact creates the threat, and how could it affect professional judgment?
2. Why is disclosing a conflict different from addressing it?
3. Give an example in which an independent reviewer helps, and one in which a reviewer cannot override a prohibition.
4. Why are independence of mind and independence in appearance both important?
5. How does the spouse-shareholding case transfer the learning from the original share gift?
6. Why can an independent team still need further audit evidence or a modified opinion?
